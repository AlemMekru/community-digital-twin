# Research Direction

## Working Research Question

How can AI-driven, explainable, and interoperable decision intelligence enhance community digital twins for evidence-based decision support?

## Initial Case Study

**Burnaby, British Columbia, Canada**

Burnaby serves as the initial real-world case study for developing and evaluating the research platform using authoritative municipal and geospatial data.

## Research Context

This research investigates how real-world community data, digital-twin state, predictive analytics, scenario simulation, and AI-driven decision intelligence can be integrated into a unified decision-support platform.

The research is particularly interested in how AI systems can operate over structured digital-twin state while maintaining:

- evidence grounding
- provenance
- explainability
- uncertainty awareness
- human oversight
- interoperability between heterogeneous community data and systems

The project will also examine interoperability and transferability between community digital-twin approaches, with particular interest in European smart-community research and Canadian municipal applications.

## Current Research Platform

The initial Burnaby implementation currently includes:

- ingestion of authoritative City of Burnaby open data
- paginated ArcGIS REST data retrieval
- validation of authoritative feature counts
- transformation of source records into digital-twin domain entities
- an initial `RoadSegment` twin entity
- automated mapping tests
- an interactive 3D representation of Burnaby
- visualization of buildings, streets, green spaces, waterways, and transit-related features
- a Next.js and TypeScript research interface

The current 3D environment is primarily a **visualization layer**. Animated vehicle and transit movement is illustrative and does not represent live City of Burnaby operational data.

Predictive models, calibrated scenario simulation, persistent digital-twin state, and AI decision-support capabilities remain under active development.

## Candidate Research Contribution Areas

The doctoral contribution is not yet fixed. Candidate areas for investigation include:

- explainable AI-driven decision intelligence over digital-twin state
- tool-grounded or agentic reasoning over community digital twins
- evidence and provenance mechanisms for AI-supported decisions
- uncertainty-aware decision support
- interoperable AI services across heterogeneous community twins
- human oversight of AI-generated recommendations
- transferability between European and Canadian community digital-twin contexts

These areas will be refined through literature review, experimentation, doctoral supervision, and evaluation.
